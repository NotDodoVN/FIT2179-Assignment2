Chapter 01 — The Root Cause

Australia's Greenhouse Gas Emissions

Australia is one of the world's highest per-capita emitters. Decades of fossil fuel dependence across energy, agriculture and transport have loaded the atmosphere with heat-trapping gases — setting every downstream crisis in motion.

Australia's trajectory versus the Paris Convention's 2030 target.

Energy generation alone accounts for roughly one-third of total emissions. Agriculture adds another 15% through livestock methane and land clearing. Without structural change, the 2030 Paris target of −43% from 2005 levels remains out of reach.

Chapter 03 — The Burning Continent

Wildfire Intensity & Frequency

Australia's bushfire seasons are growing longer, hotter, and more destructive. The 2019–20 "Black Summer" became a global symbol of climate-driven catastrophe — and it is increasingly the norm, not the exception.

MODIS satellite fire detection for H1 2026 — brightness temperature indicates fire intensity. Hotspots cluster across northern and eastern Australia.

State-by-state bushfire counts across two five-year periods. NT and Queensland consistently lead in raw count.

Chapter 04 — The Drying Wells

Water Stress & Agricultural Demand

Less rainfall, faster evaporation, and fire-damaged catchments are draining Australia's water reserves. Agriculture — which relies heavily on irrigation — consumes the vast majority of available water, setting up a dangerous collision.

Year-on-year percentage change in fire activity. Spikes correlate directly with the drought years shown above.

Australia's water use by sector in 2022–23. Agriculture accounts for over 70% of total use — making it acutely vulnerable to drought and water restrictions.

Chapter 05 — The Shrinking Harvest

Crop Production Under Pressure

The final link in the chain. Heat stress, water scarcity, and bushfire smoke combine to reduce Australia's crop yields — threatening domestic food security and export capacity.

2024–25 harvest: production volumes (bars) and year-on-year change (line) across key crops. Volatility is rising.

2025–26 projections: continued pressure on wheat, barley and canola as cumulative climate stress intensifies.

The convergence of heat, fire, and water stress is compressing crop yields in ways no single stressor could achieve alone. Australia's agricultural sector — which feeds millions domestically and exports globally — faces a structural challenge. Addressing it requires tracing the chain all the way back to its source: emissions.
